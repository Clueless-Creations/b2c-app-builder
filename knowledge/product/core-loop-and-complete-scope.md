# Core Loop And Complete Product Scope

Use this method before accepting the scope of a consumer app, whether its shape matches a
shipped archetype or requires a new implementation. A complete product can be focused. It
cannot omit a system needed to deliver its accepted promise and call that omission a later stage.

## Name the promise and core loop

Identify the user, the recurring problem, the useful outcome, the repeated action, and the
moment a user can see why this product is better than the alternatives. Select a platform
because it serves that loop, not because a starter happens to exist.

Use decisions delegated in the opening mandate. Do not ask the founder to repeat an already
supplied choice. Escalate a genuinely ambiguous product decision only when its authority was
not delegated. Record product meaning in `product.yaml`; render `PRODUCT.md`. Never write
product decisions into reducer-owned execution state.

## Enumerate every required system

Work backward from the promise and every critical journey. Name the systems required to make
them real: domain data and its lifecycle, the core interaction, persistence, recovery, privacy,
accessibility, distribution, support, and measurement. Identity, remote services, monetization,
notifications, and sharing are conditional on the accepted product, not universal defaults.

For each system specify its user outcome, dependencies, states, responsible surface, and
observable acceptance evidence. A parcel-tracking promise that requires live carrier status
cannot be fulfilled by a manually edited demo list. A subscription promise requires real purchase,
pending, cancellation, entitlement and restore behavior in the selected supported test environment.
A local-only utility need not acquire a backend simply to satisfy a generic checklist.

State explicitly whether each proposed system is required or outside the accepted promise.
An exclusion needs a rationale and a scope decision. Missing credentials, a difficult integration,
time pressure, or a working simulator shell are not reasons to reclassify required work as optional.
A blocked required system remains pending with its owner, exact missing input and resume action.
Changing the promise or removing a required system requires an explicit scope decision under the
founder's authority, not an implementation shortcut.

## Define a complete experience

Translate the core loop into a distinctive, usable experience before production implementation
hardens. Use the 11-star method to explore ambition and choose a feasible complete experience;
feasibility limits the promise, not the craft of the accepted promise. Carry that same experience
through the app, onboarding, landing page, screenshots, support and other selected surfaces.

Define meaningful interaction feedback and motion, interruption and recovery, empty/loading/error
states, accessibility and reduced-motion behavior. A quiet product may use restrained motion.
Confetti, a mascot, subscriptions, and a particular visual style are not universal requirements.
The benchmark describes quality and coherence, not a mandate to copy another app's identity.

Specify the reference evidence, native/runtime proof, independent review and repair needed for
acceptance. A neat static screenshot cannot establish interaction quality. Unit tests and a
successful compile cannot establish design quality or a functioning provider integration.

## Plan implementation without shrinking delivery

Break implementation into manageable tasks and use the simulator early for internal tests.
An internal prototype or partial build is an explicitly unfinished working artifact. It is not
the completed business promised to the founder. Do not describe required work as postponed to
another product version merely because one task or research lane has finished.

The accepted `complete_scope` in `product.yaml` names the required systems, explicit exclusions,
and acceptance conditions. Feature scope uses `required`, `excluded`, or `non-goal`. Historical
input names may be migrated by the reader, but new product writing uses the complete-scope contract.
Keep requirements and decisions in this one product owner. Detailed contracts link back to it.

## Carry the mandate through acceptance

Research completion enables independent research review, then the accepted product's design,
implementation, verification and closeout obligations. Use the active recipe and planner. Do not
create a private lane list or declare business completion from one validator's exit code.

Pricing hypotheses may be recorded as proposals. An approved production price needs offer evidence
and the applicable founder decision. Production release, publishing, spending, legal approval and
account changes remain separately authorized even when the product is otherwise ready.

## Acceptance checklist

The promise and core loop are specific. Every required system has an acceptance condition and a
real implementation plan. Exclusions do not contradict the promise. No required system is deferred
without an authorized scope change. Product decisions use the canonical product store. The design
and interaction bar is explicit, with appropriate current evidence and independent review. The
business's remaining work and protected holds stay visible after each bounded session.
